## The question

Vibe Coding is building software by describing what you want, in plain language,
and shipping what comes back. It is very good at demos. The interesting question
is not whether it can produce a demo — it obviously can — but whether it can
produce something with customers, a bill to pay and a reason to still exist in
six months.

So: **can I build a real business with Vibe Coding?**

GearNest is where I am finding out.

## Why a separate business, and not a feature of this site

GearNest is its own brand. It has its own product, its own voice and its own
roadmap, and it is not a section of my identity — it is a company I am building
that happens to be the subject of a series.

That separation matters for the honesty of the test. If GearNest were a page on
this site, "did it work" would collapse into "did I write about it". Because it
is a real product at its own address, the answer has somewhere to come from that
is not me.

## The rules of the test

Published up front, so the result cannot be quietly redefined later:

1. **The build is done the Vibe Coding way, not around it.** Where I drop out of
   it and write code by hand, that gets recorded as a point against the method,
   not edited out.
2. **The costs are published.** Tools, hosting, models, the lot. A method that
   only works with an unlimited budget has not been tested.
3. **The failures are published on the same schedule as the wins.** The parts
   that cost me a weekend are the useful half of the record.
4. **"Real business" means customers and revenue, not traffic.** Nothing else
   counts as an answer to the question.
