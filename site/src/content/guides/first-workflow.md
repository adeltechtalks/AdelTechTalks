Most automation tutorials spend fifteen minutes on theory. This one does not. You will
connect two apps, run the thing once, and see it work — then we will talk about why it
is shaped that way.

## What you need

- An account with the tool (free tier is fine)
- One app you already use every day
- Twenty minutes without interruptions

## Step 1 — Pick the boring job

The best first automation is the one you already do by hand every day and resent doing
yesterday. Not the impressive one. The boring one.

## Step 2 — Connect the trigger

The trigger is the thing that starts the workflow. Start with something that happens
often enough that you will see it fire today.

## Step 3 — Add one action

One. Not four. A workflow that does one thing reliably beats a workflow that does four
things and breaks on Tuesday.

## Step 4 — Run it once

Do not schedule it yet. Run it manually and watch what happens.

## What this costs

You will spend the first week fixing edge cases you did not think of. That is normal,
and it is still less time than doing the job by hand. The workflow that survives that
week is the one worth keeping.
