# How to use it

---

## The shape of a session

You give the engine a brief and a manifest. It gives you directions and reasons. You argue with it. That argument is the product — the engine exists to make the reasoning explicit enough to disagree with.

## A worked pass

**Brief:** compare two cameras, YouTube thumbnail, get the click, viewer should feel *challenged*.
**Manifest:** two product frames shot at matched scale and lighting; one portrait; no cutouts.

1. **Assets** — two matched product frames means comparison is available. No cutouts means collage and exploded view are out.
2. **Hero** — product A vs product B. The portrait exists but is not the subject.
3. **Beat** — comparison.
4. **Platform** — horizontal thumbnail; a bottom chrome band; judged at feed size.
5. **Response** — challenged, so the composition should pose the question rather than answer it.

**Direction 1** — Product A vs B · `/split-compare` · supporting `/feature-callout`. Matched scale either side of a hard divider, one callout on the feature under dispute, hook is a question.
**Direction 2** — A + Creator + B · `/split-compare` · supporting `/cutout-collage`. The portrait adjudicates between them. *Needs a cutout that does not exist* — so it is offered as conditional, not as a choice.

**Rejected:** Product Dominant (a comparison story with one product visible answers its own question); `/exploded-view` (no component frames); Minimal Premium (the intended response is challenge, not admiration).

## Reading the output

- **The rejections name your missing assets.** Direction 2 above tells you to go and cut out the portrait.
- **"Composition-approved, media-blocked" is a real state.** It means everything except the image is final.
- **One direction is a legitimate answer** when the assets allow one.

## Steering

Useful corrections:

- *"the portrait is weak"* → downgrade its quality in the manifest; face-led directions drop out
- *"make the viewer curious rather than informed"* → changes the tie-break, often changing the hook from a statement to a question
- *"this is for Shorts too"* → produces a recomposition, not a crop
- *"too templated"* → usually means the brand profile is prescribing layout where it should prescribe values

Unhelpful corrections: asking for more directions than the assets support, or asking it to assume an image exists.

## When to ignore it

When you can see the frame and it is better than what the rules produce. The engine encodes good defaults; it does not have taste. Record what you did instead — that is how the defaults improve.
