# /creator-floating-ui-ecosystem

**Keep a person as the human anchor while the interface they are explaining floats around them as real, readable evidence.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Mode | Static and Motion |
| Best story beats | explanation, capability, workflow, proof |
| Best content types | software features, AI tools, apps, workflows, technical explainers |
| Required assets | a clean creator frame with separable background · real UI captures or feature marks |
| Optional assets | a device in hand · one metric or state readout |
| Max combinations | one supporting skill — usually an annotation or hook skill |

## When to use it
The subject is digital and has no physical hero. A person alone cannot show what a tool does, and a screenshot alone has no one to trust. This composition solves both: the creator supplies the reason to look, the cards supply the substance.

Select it only when the content is genuinely software- or digital-feature heavy **and** the assets support it. **It is never the default.**

## When NOT to use it
There is a physical product that can carry the frame — use a product-dominant composition instead. No creator asset exists, or the background will not separate cleanly. The feature has no visual surface worth showing. Fewer than two cards carry real meaning: one honest card beats three invented ones, and at that point this is the wrong skill.

## Composition logic
The creator holds the centre vertical third and stays the largest single element. Cards sit in the side space at two or three depth tiers, nearer cards larger and crisper, farther cards smaller and softer. Cards never cross the face. Two to four cards; beyond four the frame becomes wallpaper. Each card holds one idea — an icon plus a short label, or a real captured state.

## Motion logic
Cards settle rather than fly: a short offset fade with a few pixels of drift, staggered so the eye lands on one card at a time. The creator does not move under them. Never orbit cards around a subject.

## Brand OS rules
Card surface, radius, border and glow come from the profile. Accent colour marks state or category only, never decoration. Depth and glow are used sparingly — they establish tier, not spectacle.

## Platform considerations
Mobile feed decides card count: at feed width, three cards is usually the ceiling and two is safer. Test at display size before adding a card, not after. On wide surfaces the creator may sit off-centre with cards massed on the open side.

## Arabic / English considerations
Card order follows reading direction; in RTL the first card sits on the right. Interface terms captured from a product stay in their original language — translating a UI label misrepresents the product. Body text around them follows the profile's language rules.

## Example
A person centred at a desk, three cards at two depth tiers: a captured state on one side, a feature mark and a short capability label on the other, each naming something the tool actually does.

## Anti-patterns
Decorative panels with invented labels · cards over the face · glow used as style rather than depth · more cards than the feed can resolve · a card whose text is unreadable at display size · using this for a physical product that could have carried the frame alone.
