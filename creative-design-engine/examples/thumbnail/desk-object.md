# Worked example — a collectible on a desk

A second run, deliberately small: one ordinary subject, one real photograph, no missing assets. It exists to show the engine working when nothing is blocked.

---

**Brief:** a desk-setup piece. Platform: horizontal thumbnail. Response: curious.
**Manifest:** one photograph — a collectible figure on a desk among books and a plant, phone capture, portrait orientation, **letterboxed with black bars down both sides**, strong coloured practical lighting.

## What the assets forced

Three constraints came out of the manifest before any creative choice:

1. **The bars must be cropped out.** Usable image is a known fraction of the frame, so any horizontal crop rect has a **minimum width** and an offset that puts both bars outside the frame. Arithmetic, not taste — the first pass got this wrong and the bars showed.
2. **A horizontal crop of a portrait photo cannot hold everything.** It was impossible to keep both the top of the shelf and the base of the subject. Each direction therefore decided what it was about rather than compromising into an empty middle.
3. **The lighting was already inside the brand's expressive range**, so no tint, overlay or gradient was added. The only treatment is a global grade.

## Directions

| | Composition | Skill | Hook | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Product Dominant | `/product-hero` | two words | the subject fills the frame; nothing to annotate, so nothing is annotated |
| B | Hero Object + Hook | `/product-hero` + `/sketch-overlay` | a question | the hook asks who runs the desk and **the drawn arrow answers it by landing on the figure** |
| C | Playful / Social | `/product-hero` + `/sketch-overlay` | two words | the subject is circled and given a one-word title — a punchline |

**Recommended: B.** It is the strongest *thumbnail* rather than the strongest *picture*: A is a good object shot with a label, C is a joke that lands once. B gives the viewer a reason to care about an object that is, objectively, a toy.

## What this run showed

- **The same skill pair produced three different results** because the beat and the response differed. That is the engine behaving as intended rather than as a template.
- **The hook needed a smaller type size than the profile's impact tier**, because the long word would have pushed the subject out of its own frame. The profile records that constraint as **style-specific**, to be re-measured per composition — not as a universal rule.
- **No direction was blocked**, which is what a complete manifest looks like.
